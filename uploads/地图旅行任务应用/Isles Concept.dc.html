Product brief · 2026

Footprints

A map of the small things people did in a place — made into lists you can walk, with a guide who remembers them.

Concept

The problem

Maps tell you what is somewhere. They don’t tell you what people did there — the 5:50 carillon, the $4 bánh mì on the steps, the quiet hour in the reading room. That knowledge lives in group chats and graduates with the people who had it.

The product

Anyone can make a list — “100 things before I graduate” — and pin each thing to a place. Every landmark on the map holds the traces people left there, and every trace leads back to the person and the lists behind it.

The Guide

A small wordless light that knows what happened here. It plans a route through other people’s traces, recognises your photo when you check in, and turns the day into a postcard.

Who it’s for

Students with a clock running on a place — then visitors, new arrivals and locals who want the version of a city that insiders know. Campus first, because campuses have rituals, density and a built-in reason to finish a list.

User logic

One loop. Every finished thing becomes a trace, every trace makes the map richer for the next person — which is what brings them in.

Step 6 feeds step 1 — the loop closes when someone else discovers your trace.
Three objects

Place — a landmark on the map. List — things to do, each pinned to a place. Trace — one person doing one thing at one place, with a photo and a line.

Your page

Cities you’ve collected, your stories on a map, lists you made, lists you saved — in that order. You choose which of those a visitor sees.

Trust

A check-in needs a photo the Guide recognises at that place. Ratings come only from people who finished the thing. No trace, no review.

Business model

Free to make, walk and share. Revenue comes from people who want more of the Guide, from places that want to be on a good list, and from the souvenirs a day leaves behind.

Rollout

Berkeley first, seeded with 20 lists from student ambassadors before launch. Then the next five campuses in the Bay Area, then the cities students travel to — San Francisco, Tahoe, LA.

What we watch

Traces per active user per week, and the share of new users who arrived through someone else’s trace or postcard link. That second number is the loop working.

Line we won’t cross

Sponsored lists are always labelled, and a business can never buy a place on someone else’s list or pay to hide a trace.
