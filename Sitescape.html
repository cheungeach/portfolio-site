Project · 02 · Website & iOS App

Sitescape

A social feed for personal websites. Claim your domain, post the pages you're proud of as "highlights", and let people like, save and talk about them. Every card links out to the real site — the app is the layer of attention that personal sites never had.

Concept

Everyone has a personal site. Almost no one visits them.

Two Versions · Website & App

One product, two surfaces — a website for browsing, an app for the pocket

The same prototype renders both. On the web, a left sidebar and wide two-column cards make Sitescape a place to read and discover — where you land from a shared link. On the phone, bottom tabs and a single column make it the place you check comments, post a highlight and sign a guestbook. Resize the full-screen prototype across 900px to watch it switch.

01 · Market Research · The Gap

Portfolios are built to be seen once — by a recruiter

Thirty real personal sites were collected as seed data: PMs, engineers, designers, students. Their pages range from a Notion workspace with a custom domain to a portfolio that opens as a chat window. The pattern: enormous care, almost no audience. Traffic arrives from one link in a bio, and leaves.

SitescapeA page on your own domainSite owners—

Positioning. Not a website builder and not a portfolio host. Sitescape sits on top of the site you already have: you can only post pages from a domain you've verified, and every card is an outbound link.

What we watch. Outbound clicks per highlight, and the share of highlights that receive a comment within a week. Both are shown to the owner on a stats page — the app's value is measurable by the traffic it sends away.

02 · Interaction Model · The Loop

Claim a domain, post a highlight, get visited

A highlight is one page on your site with a screenshot cover, a section tag, a tech stack and a one-line note in your voice. The feed shows highlights; tapping one opens the real page. Attention flows out, comments flow back.

02 · Interaction Model · Three Objects

Site, highlight, comment

Three objects carry the product. Identity is the domain, not a username; content is a page you already made; conversation lives on the page and in the owner's guestbook.

Site

One verified domain per person. Role tags (PM, SWE, Designer, Student…) make the feed filterable without a follow graph.

Highlight

URL + section tag + note + tech stack + cover. The cover is generated from the page kind — article, gallery, app — so the feed stays typographic even before screenshots load.

Comment

Threaded, pinnable, and mirrored into one inbox for the owner. Guestbook comments live on the profile, highlight comments on the page.

03 · UI / UX Design · Key Screens

Feed → highlight → profile → inbox

Broadsheet design system: a serif for headings, halftone covers tinted per person, whitespace instead of dividers. Cards are deliberately quiet so the sites themselves are the colour.

03 · UI / UX Design · Owner Tools

The number that matters is the one that leaves

Stats show views, likes and outbound clicks per highlight. Outbound clicks come first: the app succeeds when it sends people to your site. Onboarding is two steps — paste a URL, confirm the fetched title and screenshot — because the site already exists.

Covers

Generated placeholder covers keyed to page kind (article / gallery / app) with three tints, so a feed of thirty strangers still reads as one publication.

Responsive

One prototype, two layouts: bottom tabs on the phone, a left sidebar and wider cards on desktop.
